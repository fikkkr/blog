When I first started building school projects, I thought the goal was mostly to make the feature work. I now pay more attention to what happens when it does not work.

## A Bug Is a Question

An error is not only something to remove from the screen. It is a clue that my expectation and the program's behavior are different.

I usually start by reproducing the problem, checking the values involved, and reducing the code until the behavior becomes easier to see. Sometimes the fix is small. The understanding is the part I want to keep.

## Learning Laravel Through a Real Problem

While working on the Pesantren Attendance project, I was interested in turning a paper-based teacher-attendance process into an application. The project gave me a practical reason to learn Laravel and think about how a user action becomes stored information.

I am keeping the description simple because I am still learning the details and do not want to turn guesses into technical claims.

## The Habit I Want to Keep

I would rather understand one problem properly than collect a long list of solutions I cannot explain. Building, breaking, and rebuilding is slower sometimes, but it makes the next problem less mysterious.
